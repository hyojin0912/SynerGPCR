About SynerGPCR

SynerGPCR is an AI-augmented GPCR assay-chain database linking multi-layer functional evidence to clinical translatability. It integrates 1,753,921 assay records from 10 public sources into a mechanistically ordered chain of four assay types, covering — compounds across — GPCR targets and — approved drugs.

Assay-chain depth is synergistically predictive of clinical approval. Any single assay layer alone corresponds to clinical approval rates below 1%, whereas compounds confirmed active across all four experimental layers achieve 16.2% — a Bliss Synergy Index of 13.45 relative to the expectation under layer independence. Yet only 228 compounds are fully characterised experimentally; GPCRact AI predictions supply missing L2 or L3 states for — AI-augmented synergy candidates.

Data Construction

SynerGPCR integrates 1,753,921 assay records from ten public repositories, yielding 396,169 unique compound–GPCR pair-level chain records.

Mode-of-action (MoA) labels were assigned through a hierarchical pipeline. For structured sources (DrugBank, DrugCentral, GPCRdb), labels were mapped to a six-class schema (agonist, antagonist, partial agonist, inverse agonist, positive allosteric modulator, negative allosteric modulator). For unstructured bioassay descriptions (PubChem BioAssay, ChEMBL, BindingDB), Gemini 2.5 Flash was used with structured JSON output to parse assay type, MoA direction, and biological context. Conflicting annotations across sources were resolved by a biological-hierarchy priority scheme; 425 direct agonist–antagonist conflicts were resolved by manual review. The resulting 1,753,921 records span four mechanistically distinct assay layers.

Clinical approval status was cross-referenced across five indication databases (DrugBank, DrugCentral, TTD, IUPHAR, ChEMBL) and normalised to UMLS CUI as the master disease identifier, yielding 53,562 unique drug–disease pairs across 12 therapeutic area categories.

{# ── SECTION 3 — Assay Chain Model ── #}

Assay Chain Model and the Bliss Synergy Index

Each compound–GPCR pair is represented as an ordered chain of up to four assay layers, corresponding to successive stages of GPCR signal transduction: L1 (Receptor Binding) → L2 (G-Protein Coupling) → L3 (β-Arrestin Recruitment) → L4 (Reporter Gene Activity).

Layer Full Name Assay Types Biological Interpretation
L1 Receptor Binding Radioligand displacement (e.g. [³H] or [¹²⁵I]) and competition binding assays Confirms ligand–receptor contact. Detects binders regardless of downstream pharmacological activity.
L2 G-Protein Coupling cAMP accumulation, Ca²⁺ mobilisation, IP1 accumulation, GTPγS binding Measures second-messenger signalling immediately downstream of receptor activation, reflecting functional efficacy at the G-protein interface. The most widely available functional layer in public repositories.
L3 β-Arrestin Recruitment BRET- or HTRF-based β-arrestin recruitment Captures the G-protein-independent signalling arm. Partially sequential to, and partially competitive with, G-protein activation — making it a marker of biased agonism rather than a standalone measure of functional activity.
L4 Reporter Gene Activity Transcription factor–driven reporter gene expression Integrates the full signal-transduction cascade to the transcriptional output level. Because the reporter readout depends on sustained upstream signalling, L4 provides the highest specificity for functional agonism across the complete pathway.
Bliss Synergy Index (SI)

The clinical translatability of each chain pattern is quantified by the Bliss Synergy Index (SI), which measures how much the observed clinical approval rate exceeds the rate expected if the layers contributed independently:

SI = observed approval rate / EBliss

where EBliss = 1 − Π(1 − PLi), with PLi being the single-layer approval rate of each layer i included in the pattern. SI > 1 indicates that co-occurrence of assay activity across multiple layers is synergistically enriched relative to statistical independence.

Worked example: L1 + L2 + L4 pattern
P(L1 only) = 109 / 65,755 = 0.17%
P(L2 only) = 77 / 36,546 = 0.21%
P(L4 only) = 169 / 59,592 = 0.28%
EBliss = 1 − (1 − 0.0017)(1 − 0.0021)(1 − 0.0028) = 0.66%
Observed rate (n = 2,580 compounds): 6.40%
SI = 6.40% / 0.66% = 9.71

The Full_chain SI (13.45) is shown as a reference benchmark but should be interpreted with caution: L2 and L3 are not statistically independent (Fisher OR = 9.24, p ≈ 0), which violates the Bliss independence assumption. Primary quantitative claims use single-AI-layer panels (L1+L2+L4 and L1+L3+L4) where the two assay layers in each panel are biologically independent.

L2 (G-Protein Coupling) provides the broadest functional coverage across the compound set. L4 (Reporter Gene Activity) provides the highest specificity, as it integrates the full upstream cascade to the transcriptional output. L3 (β-Arrestin Recruitment) is treated as a distinct supplementary layer, reflecting its role as a biased agonism marker rather than a primary activity readout.

{# ── SECTION 5 — AI Integration ── #}

AI-Based Chain Completion (GPCRact)

The majority of binding-confirmed compounds lack downstream functional data: 78.5% of approved drugs have no G-protein coupling record and 96.2% lack β-arrestin data, making chain-based translatability assessment impossible from experimental data alone. To close these gaps, SynerGPCR integrates GPCRact, a structure-based E(n)-equivariant graph neural network (EGNN) that predicts L2 (G-Protein Coupling) and L3 (β-Arrestin Recruitment) activities de novo from 3D atomic structural graphs of the GPCR binding site and ligand.

GPCRact achieves balanced accuracies of 0.875 (L2, n = 395 approved drugs) and 0.834 (L3, n = 83 approved drugs) on held-out clinical test sets. When incomplete experimental chains are filled with high-confidence GPCRact predictions, the reconstructed profiles recover approximately 79% of the synergy measured on the fully experimental benchmark at both signalling layers.

For full methodology, performance metrics, and candidate lists, see the AI Prediction tab.

{# ── SECTION 7a — Comparison ── #}
Comparison with Existing Resources
SynerGPCR complements, rather than replaces, existing GPCR resources. GPCRdb provides receptor structure, generic residue numbering, and curated pharmacology; SynerGPCR provides compound-level multi-layer functional evidence aligned to clinical outcomes. ChEMBL and BindingDB supply raw bioactivity data; SynerGPCR organises these into mechanistically ordered chains and quantifies their clinical translatability.
