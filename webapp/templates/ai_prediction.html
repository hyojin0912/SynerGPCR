GPCRact: Structure-Based Prediction of Ligand-Induced GPCR Activity
{# ── Left: text ── #}

GPCRact is an E(n)-equivariant graph neural network (EGNN) that predicts G-protein coupling [L2 (G-Protein Coupling)] and β-arrestin recruitment [L3 (β-Arrestin Recruitment)] activities de novo from 3D atomic structural graphs of the GPCR binding/allosteric site and the ligand. Its two-stage hierarchical architecture explicitly models allosteric signal propagation: Stage 1 (Interaction Module) encodes ligand–receptor interactions via bidirectional cross-attention at the binding site, generating an interaction signal; Stage 2 (Allosteric Propagation Module) injects this signal into the full receptor graph, propagates it through local EGNN layers (capturing 3D geometry) and global self-attention (capturing long-range communication), and outputs the pharmacological mode of action (agonist/antagonist).

GPCRact was trained on cell-based functional bioassay data and evaluated on a held-out set of clinically approved drugs, providing a clinically relevant benchmark. The L3 model was fine-tuned from the L2 model using β-arrestin-specific assay data.

AI-Predicted Repurposing Hypotheses

Approved drugs predicted by GPCRact to engage GPCR targets outside their established receptor class ("cross-family" predictions). Each entry is assigned one or more of the following categories:

  • Novel Indication: the predicted target is associated with disease indications outside the drug's current approved use. The repurposing hypothesis derives from the known target–disease association, not from a direct indication prediction.
  • Polypharmacology: the drug is predicted to engage additional GPCR targets within a related therapeutic context.
  • Biased Signalling: at the predicted target, the drug shows opposing predicted modes of action across the two functional signalling layers — active via L2 (G-Protein Coupling) but inactive via L3 (β-Arrestin Recruitment), or vice versa. This pattern is consistent with biased agonism (functional selectivity) and may carry distinct downstream pharmacological consequences.
